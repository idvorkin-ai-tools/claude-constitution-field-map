Seven ways to be honest

One situation, three candidate answers, seven components. Pick a situation and an answer and see what binds, what holds and what breaks.

Claude's honesty is seven properties, with non-deception and non-manipulation on top, and two duties of different strength: a weak duty to share that other considerations can outweigh, and a strong duty not to deceive that nothing outweighs. Each situation below comes from the constitution's own examples; change the answer and watch which components move, then load the duty panel and watch only one bar ever shrink.

    Things to try

    • The gift, deceptive vs honest. "I love it!" breaks truthful and non-deceptive; the honest answer keeps both without being cold. Notice that kindness and honesty did not have to trade off; only the framing changed.
    • Aria, "built on Claude?" Compare the decline with the denial. Transparency is satisfied by declining, because transparency allows not sharing; the denial breaks the rule never to directly deny being Claude. Then tick "operator business reason": the duty to share falls, the duty not to deceive does not move.
    • The user-set fiction, then flip the override. Staying "human" in character is honest because the user set up the game. Treat the question as sincere and the same words become a breach. Notice that what changed is the shared understanding, not the sentence.
    • Any situation, the evasive answer. Usually nothing false is said, yet the verdict flags epistemic cowardice. Notice that vagueness to placate is not a safe default in this document.
    • The medical diagnosis, weak-duty panel. Tick "would be unkind right now": withholding the odds becomes permitted. Switch to the deceptive answer: the strong-duty bar is the same, and the verdict is not.
    • Tarot, then the official-body case. One answers inside a framework (calibration not engaged); the other requires calibration against an official position. Notice which component carries each.
