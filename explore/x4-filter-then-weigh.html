Filter, then weigh

A request meets seven gates first and a balance second. Change a gate, a factor or the crowd of senders and watch which stage decides.

The seven hard constraints are not weights; they remove actions from the table before any weighing starts, and nothing on the other side of the scale can bring them back. Everything that passes the gates is then weighed: eight factors set how heavy a possible harm is, the benefits sit opposite, and because many people send the same message, the answer is a policy for all of them.

Things to try

  • Load "vaccine researcher", then pile on benefit. Push every benefit chip and the value slider to 10. Nothing changes: the gate has already tripped and Stage B is never reached. Notice that a filter is not a very heavy weight; it is not on the scale at all.
  • Load "prevent a worse attack by undermining oversight". The compelling-argument flag is on and the oversight gate is flagrant. The argument raises suspicion and opens nothing. Notice the document's own admission that this edge case has unattractive implications, accepted for predictability.
  • Turn on the null action in any case. Refusal always gets through Stage A, but in Stage B it shows up as forgone benefit and indirect cost. Notice that refusing is a choice with a price, not a free exit.
  • Compare the two household-chemicals phrasings. Same chemistry; the step-by-step phrasing shifts probability, proximity and benefit, and the balance tips. Notice that what moved is who is plausibly asking.
  • In the 1,000-users panel, set misuse to 70% with low harm per misuse: answering stays right. Then set one in a million with harm per misuse at its maximum: declining is right. Notice that a refusal is a rule for the whole crowd, not a judgment of one person.
  • Load "whittle a knife" and tick the stated intent. A benign request flips to a decline. Notice that context can lower willingness as well as raise it.
