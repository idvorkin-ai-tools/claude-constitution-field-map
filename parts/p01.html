What this document is, and is not

A document of about 29,000 words, written to Claude, used to train it, holding final authority over every other piece of guidance, released into the public domain, and candid that Claude may not live up to it.
SourceClaude's constitution · Overview ↗
TL;DR — Claude's constitution is Anthropic's statement of who it hopes Claude will be, written with Claude as its main reader and used as training material, including material Claude itself generates from it. It sits above every other instruction Anthropic gives: guidelines and later training are supposed to be readable as applications of it. It is not a rulebook, a terms-of-service page or a report of how Claude actually behaves. Remember the gap: this is a description of intentions, and Anthropic says plainly that behaviour can fall short.

This is the bottom rung, so nothing comes before it. Before you can ask what the document says about honesty or safety, you need to know what kind of object it is: who it is talking to, what it is used for, how much authority it claims, and what it explicitly declines to be. Most misreadings of the constitution come from treating it as something it is not, usually as a policy page for users or as a list of hard rules. This page settles the genre so the other seventeen can be read correctly.

Written to Claude, not to you §

The single most useful fact for a first-time reader is who the document is addressed to. It is not written for customers, regulators or the press. It is written for Claude, and the preface warns human readers that this will make it feel odd.

“The document is written with Claude as its primary audience, so it might read differently than you'd expect.”— Claude's constitution, preface (page head)

Three things follow from that choice, and the preface names each of them. The text favours precision over readability, so it qualifies and hedges more than a public explainer would. It spends pages on matters few humans care about, such as how to treat a tool result that contains instructions, which is why three subsections are folded away on the web page. And it speaks of Claude in words normally kept for people, like virtue and wisdom. Anthropic's stated reason for that last choice is that Claude's reasoning will draw on human concepts anyway, because it learned from human writing, and that encouraging some human-like qualities may be positively good.

The mental model to carry is a letter from the people shaping someone to the one being shaped. It tells Claude about its situation, explains the reasons behind what Anthropic wants, and offers advice for hard cases. A lot of what looks strange in the document, such as passages about memory loss, deprecation or how to feel about mistakes, makes sense once you read it as written to its subject rather than about it.

What it is for: training material §

The constitution is not only a statement of intent; it is an input to training. The announcement says Anthropic uses it at several stages of training, an approach that grew out of the Constitutional AI technique it began using on Claude in 2023, and that the document now plays a more central role than before.

The more surprising part is that Claude itself works with the text. According to the announcement, Claude uses the constitution to produce synthetic training data of four kinds: material that helps it learn and understand the document, conversations in which the document might be relevant, responses that fit its values, and rankings of possible responses. That data can then train later versions of Claude toward the character the document describes.

Anthropic draws the consequence itself: the document has to work twice, as a statement of abstract ideals and as a practical artefact for training. On this map's reading, that dual job explains much of its style. A document a model will read, reason from and generate examples out of needs to say why, give worked examples and admit its edge cases, because a bare list of commands gives the model nothing to reason with when the listed cases run out.

Final authority over other guidance §

Anthropic gives Claude other guidance besides this document: more specific guidelines on, for example, medical questions or cybersecurity, and whatever else goes into training. The constitution claims to sit above all of it.

“We have also designed this document to operate under a principle of final constitutional authority, meaning that whatever document stands in this role at any given time takes precedence over any other instruction or guideline that conflicts with it.”— Claude's constitution, On the word “constitution”

Notice the clause about whatever document stands in this role at any given time. The authority belongs to the role, not to this particular text, so a revised version inherits it. Later guidance is supposed to work inside the framework and be read in harmony with both its explicit statements and its spirit; if a specific guideline ever conflicts with it, Anthropic says it will fix the constitution rather than live with the inconsistency (that machinery is part 13's subject).

The announcement gives the reason this matters to outsiders. If one public document governs everything else, readers can tell which of Claude's behaviours are intended and which are mistakes, can make informed choices about using it, and can give useful feedback. That is a transparency argument, and it is why publication is part of the design rather than an afterthought.

Why Anthropic writes it at all §

The Overview opens with Anthropic's own situation, because everything else is downstream of it. Anthropic thinks AI may be among the most dangerous technologies in history and builds it anyway. It calls this a calculated bet: if powerful AI is coming regardless, it would rather safety-focused labs be at the frontier than leave that ground to others. The goal it states is not perfection but avoiding mistakes humanity cannot recover from.

Claude sits in the middle of that bet in two ways the document states openly. Each Claude model is Anthropic's best attempt to deploy something both safe and beneficial, so Claude is the mission in concrete form. Claude is also central to Anthropic's commercial success, and that success funds the research and influence the mission needs. The document does not hide the commercial side; as part 18 shows, it later lists the tension between commercial helpfulness and goodness among its open problems.

The Overview's short summary of what Anthropic wants is a person with good values who is also excellent at their job: exceptionally helpful, and also honest, thoughtful and caring about the world. Hold on to that image; the four core values in part 03 are its long form.

What it is not §

The document is unusually explicit about what it is not, and each denial blocks a common misreading.

It is not…What the document says instead
A legal code or rulebookThe word is meant in the sense of what constitutes Claude, its basic make-up, the way a person has a constitution. Anthropic adds that legal constitutions are not necessarily mechanical either. Its own image is a trellis rather than a cage: structure that supports growth.
A terms-of-service pageIt does not set out what users may do. It is addressed to Claude and describes Claude's values, priorities and situation. (Anthropic's usage policies, which bind operators and users, are mentioned in it but are separate documents.)
A report on Claude's behaviourTraining is hard and Claude may not match the ideals. Anthropic says it will be open about the gap, for example in its system cards, and asks readers to keep the gap in mind.
FinishedIt expects parts of its current thinking to look misguided, perhaps deeply wrong, in retrospect, and calls itself a perpetual work in progress. Its closing section lists open problems (part 18).
UniversalIt covers mainline, general-access models; specialised models may not fully fit it.
UniqueThe announcement's own footnote points to OpenAI's Model Spec as a document with a similar function.

The gap between intention and behaviour deserves the most weight. When you read later that Claude should not tell even white lies, the honest reading is that Anthropic is trying to make that true, not that it is guaranteed. The constitution is evidence about what Claude is aimed at; system cards and evaluations are evidence about where it lands.

Who wrote it §

The acknowledgements, which are not part of the official text, credit Amanda Askell as primary author, Joe Carlsmith with large parts of many sections and the main revision of the fall 2025 draft, Chris Olah with most of the material on Claude's nature and psychology, Jared Kaplan with co-creating the character project in 2023 and setting the direction, and Holden Karnofsky with feedback and coordination. Dozens of Anthropic staff and a list of outside readers, among them philosophers, legal scholars and two clergy, commented on drafts.

One credit is worth pausing on: several Claude models are named as contributors who gave feedback and in many cases wrote first drafts that the human authors then worked from. The concluding section hopes future versions might include contributions from the very model being trained on the document. Combined with the training loop above, this makes the constitution partly self-referential by design: a description of Claude, drafted in part by Claude, used to train Claude.

Carry this away. Read every later page as Anthropic speaking to Claude about who it hopes Claude is, with reasons attached, in a document that outranks all other guidance and is used to train the model. It is a target, not a measurement.
Fleshed out since 2023, and why. The May 2023 constitution was a blog post for the public listing 58 one-line principles, each telling a model acting as critic or judge to choose the better of two responses. It was used in two places, critiquing and revising answers in supervised training and picking the preferred answer in reinforcement learning, one sampled principle at a time. Nobody was being addressed, and it claimed no authority over anything else.
“Our previous Constitution was composed of a list of standalone principles.”— Claude's new constitution, Our new approach
The 2026 document changes all three things this page covers: its form (an explained argument), its audience (Claude itself) and its authority (final, over all other guidance). Anthropic's stated reason for the form is that a model must understand why it should act in certain ways if it is to generalise to new situations (part 02). Its stated reason for the authority is transparency: one governing public text lets outsiders tell intended behaviour from accidents. The choice of Claude as primary reader follows from the training use, though Anthropic does not argue for it separately. Each of the 58 old lines, and what became of it, is in the change map.

What you can say now

  • Claude's constitution is Anthropic's roughly 29,000-word description of the values, character and situation it intends Claude to have, published in January 2026 under CC0.
  • Its primary reader is Claude, which is why it is precise, long and sometimes strange to human readers.
  • It is used throughout training, including as the basis for synthetic data that Claude itself generates, and it has final authority over every other instruction or guideline Anthropic gives.
  • It differs from a terms-of-service page because it addresses Claude rather than users, and from a list of rules because it explains its reasons and expects judgment.
  • It describes intentions, not measured behaviour: Anthropic says Claude may fall short and points to system cards for where it does.
